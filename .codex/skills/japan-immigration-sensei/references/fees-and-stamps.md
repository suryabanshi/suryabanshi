# Government fees & 収入印紙 (revenue stamps)

> Fees are set by the government and change periodically. **Always confirm the current amount**
> on the Immigration Services Agency (出入国在留管理庁) website before quoting a number to a person.
> The figures below are typical historical amounts, shown to explain the mechanism — not a
> guaranteed current price.

## How payment usually works
Most in-Japan immigration procedures charge the fee **only when the application is approved**,
paid with **収入印紙 (revenue stamps)**. You:
1. Buy the revenue stamp(s) for the exact fee — at the immigration counter, a post office, or
   some convenience stores.
2. Affix them to the fee-payment slip you receive on approval.
3. Some procedures can also be paid **online** via the residence-application system.

## Typical fee mechanics (verify current amounts)
| Procedure | Fee mechanism |
|---|---|
| Extension of period of stay (在留期間更新) | Revenue-stamp fee on approval (commonly quoted around ¥4,000) |
| Change of status (在留資格変更) | Revenue-stamp fee on approval (commonly quoted around ¥4,000) |
| Permanent residence (永住許可) | Higher revenue-stamp fee on approval (commonly quoted around ¥8,000) |
| COE issuance (在留資格認定証明書) | No issuance fee for the COE itself |
| Visa at embassy/consulate abroad | Separate visa fee set by MOFA / the consulate; paid there |
| JAPAN eVISA | Visa fee paid online during the eVISA process |

## Being transparent about money
If this skill is used inside any paid service:
- State the **government fee** and the **service/preparation fee** as two separate line items.
- Never imply the government fee is larger than it is, and never collect the government fee on
  the person's behalf as though it were a private charge — the person pays it at the counter or
  through the official online system.
- The sensei's spirit is to keep the person's cost to the true government fee wherever possible.
